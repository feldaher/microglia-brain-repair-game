The tissue moves by the computer model of El-Daher et al. 2024 (Life Science Alliance), with its published settings and the cell layout of its Fig. 4A. Each pulling microglia is tied to every other cell, however far, as if through the mesh of astrocyte fibres that fills the tissue. The skin does not give; being tied to it keeps the microglia out in the open space, over the wound. The model is a flat slice, drawn about four times larger than the real tissue. Time runs times faster than life. “Wound closed” is the share of the boxed region that neurons have covered; in the paper the wound was outlined by hand, so these numbers are not the paper's.

Close the Wound.

A needle has gone through the brain of a young zebrafish. In a day the wound will be shut: not by new cells, but by the brain's immune cells, the microglia, pulling the tissue together. You are one of them.

  • CrawlClick the tissue and your yellow cell goes there.
  • PullPress Space to hold on. The tissue creeps toward you.
  • CallPress C to bring in another microglia. One is not enough.
  • LookTwenty hours pass in a minute. Click a label for the real thing.
